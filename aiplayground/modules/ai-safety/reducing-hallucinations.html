Reducing Hallucinations

Hallucinations can be reduced by giving the AI reliable information, clearer instructions, and permission to say when it does not know. But reducing hallucinations does not guarantee that every answer will be correct.

Confidence vs Evidence

Sounding sure is not the same as being right

Two answers to the same maintenance question — one sounds confident, the other shows its source.

Confident but Unsupported
“The equipment must be replaced every five years.”
ConfidenceHigh
EvidenceNone
→ Do not trust automatically.
Cautious and Supported
“The maintenance policy states inspection is required every five years. It does not say replacement is mandatory.”
ConfidenceModerate
EvidenceMaintenance Policy, Section 4
→ More trustworthy.
Confidence is how certain an answer sounds. Evidence is what supports it — and evidence matters more than confident wording.
Important Limitations

These methods reduce risk, not remove it

Even a grounded answer can still be wrong. Here is what can still go wrong:

The source itself may be wrong.
The source may be outdated.
The wrong document may be retrieved.
The AI may misunderstand the context.
The answer may combine facts incorrectly.
A source reference may not fully support the claim.
Grounded does not automatically mean correct.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation can reduce unsupported answers, but it is not a complete guarantee against hallucinations.

Key Takeaways

  • • Give the AI trusted and relevant context.
  • • Use clear instructions that discourage guessing.
  • • Let the AI say when information is missing.
  • • Ask for evidence or sources.
  • • Do not confuse confidence with correctness.
  • • Verify important answers against the original source.
  • • Hallucinations can be reduced, but not completely eliminated.

Summary

Hallucinations are reduced when AI answers are grounded in trusted information, guided by clear instructions, and checked against evidence. When the facts are missing, the safest answer may be to say so.
