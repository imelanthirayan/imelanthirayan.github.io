Guardrails

Guardrails are checks and rules placed around an AI system to control what it receives, produces, or does. They do not change what the model knows — they control how the application handles risky situations.

Guardrails vs Model

Two different jobs

Model Behaviour

The model decides what response is likely to come next.

Generates text
Follows instructions
Uses context
May still make mistakes
Guardrails

The application checks whether the request, response, or action should be allowed.

Filters unsafe inputs
Checks outputs
Limits tool access
Requires approval
The model creates the response. Guardrails control how the application handles it.
Limitations

Guardrails reduce risk — they don't remove it

Guardrails control behaviour, but they do not guarantee correctness.

Key Takeaways

  • • Guardrails are checks placed around an AI system.
  • • Input guardrails check user requests.
  • • Output guardrails check AI responses.
  • • Action guardrails control what AI can do.
  • • They may allow, block, rewrite, review, or require approval.
  • • Guardrails do not replace evaluation or human oversight.
  • • Multiple layers are stronger than one rule.

Summary

Guardrails are rules and checks that control what an AI system receives, produces, and does. They reduce risk by blocking, changing, or reviewing unsafe situations, but they are only one part of AI safety.
